Launch Roadmap V1

27 Sept 2026 · Daniel

V1 launches as a complete loop: learn, compete, level up, show it off. It covers four areas (Learn, Compete, Events, Progression) tied together by one XP and badge system, with a separate leaderboard for each area.

Goal and quality bar

The one thing V1 must nail: a live coding match (1v1 or Territory) that feels fast, fair and worth sharing. Everything else feeds that loop or gives people a reason to come back tomorrow.

Principles

  • Skill and effort are measured separately. Ratings show how good you are and can go down. XP shows how much you play and only goes up.
  • Every area keeps its own points and its own leaderboard, so a strong learner and a strong duelist can both be at the top of something.
  • Never sell an advantage in competition. Ratings, ranked games and leaderboard placement stay free.
  • Every check happens in Convex before data leaves the server.

Quality bar

Measure Target
Landing page to first match Under 3 minutes
Verdict after Submit Under 3 seconds
Wrong verdicts Zero: judging correctness is the reputation
XP, badges and ratings Never double-counted, never lost
Themes Light and dark
Phones Marketing, profiles, leaderboards and challenges work; solving is desktop-first

What changed from the lean plan

The lean cut shipped only 1v1 and practice. V1 now also ships Territory, courses and roadmaps, daily and weekly challenges, company-run ninjathons, full profiles and a progression system. That roughly doubles the build, so the build order and risks sections matter more.

V1 scope: Learn

Practice and tutorials are free; structured courses and roadmaps are Pro, with the first module free.

  • Problem library: 150 to 200 seeded problems, the "Solving in" language switch, status per language, collections, filters and topic chips.
  • Solve view: Run and Submit, live verdicts, 9 languages: JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Java, C#, C++ and Rust ranked; HTML and CSS practice-only until V1.1.
  • Docs library: Python and MDN at launch, pinned to the runner's versions; more doc sets later.
  • Tutorials (free): short standalone lessons such as "Two pointers" or "Hash maps in Python", each ending in 2 or 3 library problems. They are public pages, so they also bring search traffic.
  • Roadmaps (Pro, first module free): a visual path of topics from beginner to interview-ready. Each node is a lesson plus practice problems, with progress saved per node.
  • Courses (Pro, first module free): a guided sequence inside a roadmap or language, for example "Learn Rust through 40 problems". Exercises stay in the public library; the teaching around them is Pro.
  • Launch content: 3 roadmaps (Programming basics, Data structures and algorithms, Interview prep) and about 20 tutorials. Hand-written hints on every course problem, so learning works before the AI coach exists.
  • Learning points: lessons, modules and finished roadmaps earn XP and feed a Learning leaderboard.

V1 scope: Compete

Two match modes at launch, each with its own rating and leaderboard: 1v1 race and Territory.

1v1 race

  • Find a match: always ranked. Matched by 1v1 rating, the range widening the longer you wait.
  • Challenge someone: by username or link. The challenger picks Ranked or Unranked, and the invite shows the choice before the other player accepts.
  • Ranked challenges have limits, so friends can't farm rating off each other: both players need 10 ranked games, the rating gap must be under 400, and only the first 3 ranked games between the same two players per day change ratings.
  • Ghost races when the queue is empty: race a recorded real solve of a player near your rating, clearly labelled as a ghost, no rating change.
  • Scoring: 1v1 rating (Glicko-2), plus wins, losses, win rate, current and best win streak, and fastest solves on the profile.

Territory (3 to 6 players)

  • 37-region hex map for 3 to 4 players, 61 for 5 to 6. Home bases can't be captured, new captures are shielded for 60 seconds, first to more than half the points wins, or the most points at 30 minutes.
  • Find a match: ranked. Challenge someone: ranked or unranked, same limits as 1v1.
  • Scoring: a separate Territory rating using a multiplayer rating system (OpenSkill, open source), plus regions captured, cores held, placements and wins.
  • Office and friend groups: free private groups joined by invite code. Each group gets its own leaderboards and a "start a Territory lobby with the group" button. This is what makes Territory work in week one without a big queue.

V1 scope: Challenges and ninjathons

Daily and weekly challenges give everyone a shared reason to show up; ninjathons are bigger events that only we create in V1.

  • Daily and weekly challenges are free and never change the 1v1 or Territory rating; they have their own points.
  • Streaks: a daily streak counter with one "freeze" earned per 7-day streak, so one missed day doesn't erase a month.
  • Ninjathons in V1: an internal admin page to create an event (brief, dates, team size, rubric, prizes), team registration, submission by repository link plus a short demo, judging by our team, and a public results page. Plan one launch ninjathon about 4 weeks after launch.
  • Weekly contests (live, 90 minutes, contest rating) wait for V1.1; weekly challenges cover the "every week" moment until then.

V1 scope: Progression

Yes to a leveling system: XP and levels reward effort across every area, while each area keeps its own skill points. The two never mix, so XP can't buy a better rating.

Ninjathon 200 for submitting, 500 to 1,000 for placing Once per event

Levels and titles: level n needs 100 × n^1.5 XP in total, which makes early levels come fast and later ones slow. Each title band unlocks a profile badge and, at 20 and 40, a profile theme.

Titles use different words from the rating tiers (Expert, Master and so on), so nobody mixes up "plays a lot" with "plays well".

Badges: grouped by area, each showing its rarity (the share of players who hold it).

  • Milestones: first solve, 100 and 500 solves, first ranked win.
  • Streaks: 7, 30 and 100 days.
  • Skill: reaching each rating tier, a comeback win, holding the Core at the end of a Territory match.
  • Learning: first tutorial, each finished roadmap.
  • Events: daily challenge streaks, weekly top 10%, ninjathon participant and winner.
  • Languages: 50 problems solved in one language.

V1 scope: Profiles, social and growth

Profiles ship complete in V1: a strong default for everyone and full customization for Pro.

  • Default profile (free): avatar, bio, links, languages, level and title, the fixed stats block (1v1 and Territory ratings, rank, record, solved, streak), badges, activity grid, pinned solutions, recent matches.
  • Themes: 3 free, more earned through levels and rating tiers, the full template library with Pro.
  • Custom profile (Pro): accent color, banner, heading font, section order and visibility, custom URL. Theme values only, never user CSS, with a contrast check.
  • Friends and groups: follow players, create or join a group by invite code, see friends online.
  • Share cards: an image card for every match result, level-up, badge and finished roadmap, sized for X, LinkedIn and Discord.
  • Challenge links: work for people without an account, who sign up and land straight in the match. This is the main growth loop.
  • Notifications: incoming challenges, a daily-challenge reminder, streak at risk, badge earned. Email plus in-app, each switchable.

V1 scope: Trust, platform and Pro at launch

Pro launches with V1 because courses, roadmaps and custom profiles are paid, so payments and entitlements are part of the first build.

Trust and platform

  • Sandboxed runner on Vercel Sandbox: a fresh microVM per run, no network, strict CPU, memory, time and output limits; Convex waits for the output and decides the verdict.
  • Convex guardrail: every public function built from userQuery / proQuery wrappers; hidden tests and unstarted match problems never leave the server; the server clock decides all timing.
  • Anti-cheat: tab-leave and paste signals, provisional ratings, similarity checks after ranked games, the ranked-challenge limits, flagged games reviewed rather than auto-punished.
  • Progression integrity: one append-only XP ledger where every entry has a unique key per action, so a retried request can never award XP twice. Badges and levels are computed from the ledger.
  • Marketing page, public SEO pages for problems, tutorials, profiles and leaderboards, sitemap and Open Graph images.
  • GDPR: tracking disclosed, raw behavior events deleted after 90 days, account deletion.

After V1

Each release is its own launch moment, which brings a new wave of attention instead of one.

V1.1 adds what people pay for next (the coach) and a live weekly event; V1.2 opens creation to Pro users; V2 brings the business side and the big multiplayer modes.

Build order

Build the judging core first, then the foundation and the progression engine, because every later feature runs code through the runner and writes XP, badges and points into the engine. Each phase ends with a check that must pass before the next one starts. Revised 3 Oct 2026 (see notes/decisions.md).

Content track, alongside the build from phase 2: problems grow from about 30 to 150–200 by phase 10. Tutorials and roadmaps are written before phase 7, and weekly sets before phase 5.

What changed from the first build order: the foundation is split into the judging core and the rest; every phase has a check that marks it done; content is its own track; daily and weekly challenges come before Territory because they are cheaper and need no other players online; a private alpha follows 1v1 instead of waiting for the closed beta; and the runner load test moves from launch readiness to 1v1.

Risks and open decisions

The biggest risk is scope: this V1 is about twice the lean cut, so the quality bar has to hold even if a feature slips.

Risk What it looks like Mitigation
Scope Launch keeps moving, or everything ships half-done If time runs short, cut in this order: ninjathons to the first month after launch, then weekly challenges, then courses beyond the first roadmap. Never cut judging quality.
Content load Roadmaps, tutorials and weekly sets need writing every week Daily challenges picked automatically; write 6 weekly sets before launch; start with 3 roadmaps
Cold start Empty queues, especially for Territory's 3 players Ghost races for 1v1; a private alpha with friends after 1v1; groups and challenge links for Territory; office teams in the closed beta
Rating boosting Friends farming ranked challenges Minimum 10 ranked games, rating gap under 400, 3 counted games per pair per day, similarity checks
XP farming Grinding easy problems or duels for levels First-solve-only XP, per-opponent caps, XP never affects ratings
Payment bugs Paid users locked out or free users getting Pro Entitlements written only by the webhook; a test per Pro function that calls it as a free user

Decided (3 Oct 2026, details in notes/decisions.md)

  • Usernames: our own, unique and chosen by each user in onboarding, stored in Convex (not Clerk).
  • Payments: Stripe directly, not Clerk Billing. Stripe Tax or a merchant of record is still to choose.
  • Judging model: a hybrid. Each problem is either function mode (fill in a generated signature) or full-program stdin/stdout mode. A "write the whole program" toggle on function problems comes after V1.
  • Code runner: Vercel Sandbox, called from Convex; the desktop app runs code locally later.
  • Brand name: Ninjathons, with a shuriken logo and a neon green accent.
  • Languages: all 9 in V1 (JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Java, C#, C++, Rust ranked; HTML and CSS practice-only until ranked CSS duels in V1.1).
  • Docs in ranked 1v1: official language references only; no-docs play is a separate Hard mode.
  • Pro price: about €8/month or €69/year, regional prices, early-bird pricing kept when it rises with V1.1.
  • World Conquest and Battle royale: keep both; choose the order in V2 by how many players are online at once.
  • Build plan: 11 phases (0 to 10), each with a check that marks it done; the judging core comes first, daily and weekly challenges move before Territory, a private alpha follows 1v1, and content is its own track.
